Push notifications without spam: retention tactics that feel useful

Push notifications without spam: retention tactics that feel useful — Altmash's practical guide with steps, mistakes to avoid, key metrics and a 30-day plan.

  • Validate one core job before building more.
  • Ship small updates often.

What is really at stake with Android app development

India is a mobile-first market where Android dominates, so an app can become your most direct line to customers. But an app only pays off if people open it again after the first day.

Most app budgets are lost after launch: installs bought through ads that never become active users because onboarding, speed or the core feature did not match the promise.

The good news is that none of this needs a big team. It needs clear decisions, a weekly rhythm and someone who owns the result after launch — which is exactly how I run Android app development for the brands I manage.

A step-by-step process for Android app development for startups

Here is the process I follow. The order matters: each step removes a reason for the next one to fail.

  • Validate the core use case: one job the app must do better than a website or WhatsApp.
  • Scope an MVP: the smallest feature set that delivers that job, with a fixed release date.
  • Design onboarding: get users to the first useful moment within a minute.
  • Build for Indian devices: test on low and mid-range phones, limited storage and slow networks.
  • Instrument analytics: events for signup, first key action and retention from day one.
  • Launch and iterate: Play Store listing optimisation, feedback loops and fortnightly releases.

Give each step a clear owner and a deadline. A plan without owners is just a list of good intentions, and Android app development punishes good intentions that never ship.

What to measure

Pick a small set of numbers and review them every week in the same place. I prefer five metrics the whole team understands over a dashboard nobody opens.

  • Day 1, Day 7 and Day 30 retention
  • Activation rate to the first key action
  • Crash-free sessions
  • Cost per install versus cost per active user
  • Play Store rating and review sentiment

Look at trends over four to eight weeks rather than daily swings, and always tie the numbers back to revenue or qualified leads.

Budget and timeline: what to expect

An MVP Android app in India commonly costs ₹1,50,000 to ₹6,00,000 depending on features, integrations and design, with eight to sixteen weeks to a first release. Budget separately for maintenance and updates after launch.

These ranges are starting points, not quotes. The right number depends on your category, your margins and how much of the work your team can handle in-house. What matters most is committing to a test long enough to learn from it.

The Indian market context

Indian buyers are mobile-first, value-conscious and quick to message a brand on WhatsApp before they buy. Page speed on patchy networks, clear pricing, easy payment options and fast replies are part of Android app development, not separate from it.

Festive seasons, regional preferences and cash-on-delivery habits also shape results. Plan your calendar around them instead of treating every month the same.

Where most brands go wrong with Android app development

These are the mistakes I see most often when I audit brands. Each one looks small on its own; together they explain why results stall.

  • Building every feature before validating the core one.
  • Onboarding that asks for too much before showing value.
  • A heavy app size that low-end phones cannot keep installed.
  • Buying installs before measuring retention.
  • Play Store screenshots that do not match the real app.

If you recognise two or more of these in your own business, fix them before adding budget. More spend on a broken system only makes the problem more expensive.

Tools and setup I recommend

You do not need expensive software to do this well. This is the stack I usually start with:

  • Kotlin or Flutter for development
  • Firebase for analytics, crash reporting and push notifications
  • Google Play Console for releases and store listing experiments
  • Figma for UI design and prototypes
  • Emulators plus real low-end test devices

Tools only help when someone owns them. Assign one person to each tool, document the setup, and keep every login in the brand's name rather than a freelancer's personal account.

Putting it into action: a 30-day plan

If you want to move quickly, here is how I would sequence the first month.

  • Week 1: Validate the core use case and scope an MVP.
  • Week 2: Design onboarding.
  • Week 3: Build for Indian devices and instrument analytics.
  • Week 4: Launch and iterate, then review your metrics and decide what to keep.

At the end of the month you will have real data instead of opinions — and a clear picture of where to invest next.

Final thoughts

If you take one thing from this guide, let it be this: Android app development compounds when it is treated as a system. Fix the inputs, keep a simple scoreboard and give each change enough time to show results.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to build an Android app in India?

A simple MVP often starts around ₹1,50,000, while apps with payments, real-time features or complex backends cost significantly more. Scope is the biggest cost driver.

Should I build a website or an app first?

If users need the product occasionally, start with a fast mobile website. Build an app when users need frequent, repeated access, notifications or device features.

Flutter or native Kotlin?

Flutter is efficient when you want Android and iOS from one codebase. Native Kotlin can be better for performance-heavy features or deep Android integrations. Choose based on your roadmap, not the trend.

What is the first thing to fix in Android app development?

Start with clarity: who it is for, what the offer is and what action you want next. Most Android app development problems that look technical are really unclear decisions made earlier.
